The Man & The Method

Golden-Era Thinking

Who Nash is, and the single idea underneath everything he teaches.

Dr. Nash Jocic is a 64-year-old bodybuilding coach with 40+ years under the bar — 46 contests, 17 titles, and by his own count more than 3,000 clients trained. He built his approach on the Golden Era: Serge Nubret (whom he met in Paris around 1982), Vince Gironda, Arnold, Sergio Oliva, Frank Zane and Lee Haney. His verdict on most modern training and nutrition advice is blunt — it's wrong, and the classic-era greats had it right.

His own arc frames the whole system: he started on Mike Mentzer's one-set-to-failure "Heavy Duty" plus a high-carb diet, and got fat and injured. After meeting Nubret he flipped to high volume, short rest, high protein and low carbs — and never looked back. The method below is aimed squarely at the natural lifter, who (unlike the enhanced pros) can't cheat recovery or nutrition.

Tension is the driver

Mechanical tension is the #1 growth signal — then metabolic stress, then muscle damage. The pump is a by-product, not the goal.

Aesthetics over mass

Bodybuilding ≠ powerlifting. He chases the V-taper, small waist and proportion of the classic physiques. "Muscle is the end; weight is only the means."

Hormones over calories

"A calorie is not a calorie." How food moves insulin matters more than how many calories it carries. Insulin sensitivity is the master switch.

"Consistency beats intensity — every single time."

I
Part One · The Gym Plan

How Nash Trains

Once a week per muscle, high volume, stopping just shy of failure — built to be repeated for decades without injury.

Frequency & Split

Hit Each Muscle Once Every 7 Days

His single most-repeated rule. A muscle recovers in 48–72 h, but the connective tissue (tendons, ligaments, fascia) and the nervous system around it need 5–7 days. You train a whole "unit," and super-compensation peaks around day 5–7 — so twice-a-week frequency (or PPL six days a week) quietly accumulates injury for a trained lifter.

The Cornerstone Train a muscle. Then leave it alone for a week.

Sessions run 45–60 minutes. Beginners start with full-body 3×/week, graduate to upper/lower 2×/week, and only then move to the once-a-week split below.

Volume & Reps

Go High-Volume — Then Cap It

This is the "Serge Nubret conversion." Naturals benefit more from volume than the pros do. Build up to it: it's a target, not a starting point.

20–30sets · legs / back / chest
16–20sets · shoulders
12–16sets · biceps / triceps
10–12sets · beginner start

Reps by body part

Upper body 8–12. Quads / legs 12–20+ (advanced 20–25). Calves 12–20. Never drop below 8 upper / 12 for quads — it shortens time-under-tension and courts injury.

"Junk volume" ≠ high volume

Junk = sets taken to failure, sets too heavy, partial reps, pyramid warm-ups. Done properly (near-failure + short rest), the body self-limits total quality sets to about 20–30.

Evolution flagged: in his earliest 2024 videos he cited a research-based ~10–20 sets; through 2025 he settled firmly on the higher 20–30 range.

Intensity & Failure

Stop 1–2 Reps Short — Never Train to Failure

Where the growth is Leave 1–2 reps in the tank on every set.

The last ~5–6 reps before failure are the "quality" reps. Stopping just short lets you do many more sets — so you accumulate more quality reps overall (≈100 across 20 near-failure sets vs. ≈60 across 10 sets to failure), without frying the nervous system, joints and tendons.

✕The Myth

Train to absolute failure for maximum growth.

✓Nash

Failure fatigues the whole system and causes injury. Take at most one set per exercise to failure, occasionally. "Stimulate, don't annihilate" (Lee Haney).

He is firmly anti-HIT / Heavy Duty: he argues even Mentzer and Dorian Yates actually trained ~20–25 sets, and that one-set-to-failure "was a marketing tool." For naturals: no forced reps, negatives, drop sets or supersets as a staple (drop sets maybe monthly).

Rest & Tempo

Short Rest, Slow Negatives

30–60srest · upper body

60–120 s for legs. Long 3–5 min rests train the nervous system — that's powerlifting, not hypertrophy.

3–4sper rep tempo

≈1–2 s up, 2 s+ down. No bouncing, no lockout — keep continuous tension.

60–70%of growth = the eccentric

"Bodybuilding is more about lowering the weight." Muscles handle ~40% more load lowering than lifting.

Target set duration: ≈20–40 s upper body, 45–90 s legs. "Every next set steps on the tail of the previous one" — rest only until breathing is under control and form holds.

Straight Sets & Progressive Overload

Same Weight Every Set — Earn the Next Plate

Straight sets, not pyramids

Use the same weight for every working set, each taken near failure, reps naturally dropping (e.g. 100 kg × 12, 10, 8). It's trackable — critical for a natural. Pyramids waste the first sets as warm-ups and leave you fatigued for the one heavy set.

The overload rule

Adding weight every week is ego lifting. Add ~5% (or 2.5 kg) only after you beat the top of the rep range in perfect form — then reps reset to the bottom. Raise reps, tempo, sets and form before load. Muscle grows in waves, not a straight line.

✕The Myth

Put more weight on the bar every single week.

✓Nash

True overload is mechanical tension, not load. Micro-progress over months and years — that's how you add up to ~10 kg of muscle.

Form principle that ties it together

Full range with a loaded stretch ("passive tension") is the key growth stimulus — except where the stretch carries no tension (barbell/DB curls, DB lateral & bent-over raises), where a machine or cable supplies it instead. Control the negative, squeeze where the movement allows, don't lock out, and initiate every rep with the target muscle.

Cardio · Recovery · Who It's For

Walk, Sleep, Repeat

Skip cardio — walk instead

Conventional cardio raises cortisol and eats muscle on low carb. High-volume, short-rest lifting is your cardiovascular work. For everything else: outdoor walking, 30–60 min, most days, medium pace, nasal breathing, ideally fasted and separate from the workout.

Recovery is training

Sleep 7–9 h in a dark, cool room before midnight — growth hormone releases ~60–90 min in. Watch for "survival mode" (growth shut down) from chronic stress, high insulin, CNS fatigue, poor sleep or inflammation. No deload needed if you train right.

Over 40 / 50 / 60

You can build muscle at any age. Keep the once-a-week 4-day split, avoid failure and maximal weights, warm up longer (shoulders 5–10 min), eat more protein, and guard recovery. Women: same system.

II
Part Two · The Nutrition Plan

How Nash Eats

High protein, plenty of fat, few or no carbs, two or three meals a day — engineered around insulin, not calories.

The Macro Blueprint

Protein First, Fat Second, Carbs Last

A high-protein, low-carb, animal-based template. The target ratio is roughly 2 parts protein to 1 part fat by grams, with carbs low or absent — e.g. a 300 g protein day sits near 150 g fat and 0–100 g carbs.

Protein · 2–3 g/kg Fat · ½× protein Carbs
Protein — the priority Fat — essential, ~half of protein Carbs — optional, <50–150 g
✕The Myth

Count calories; a calorie is a calorie.

✓Nash

What food does to your hormones — chiefly insulin — matters more than the number. He doesn't count; he follows hunger and principle.

Protein

Eat More Than They Tell You — With Fat

2–3g/kgbodyweight · naturals

Over-40s more; enhanced pros 4–6 g/kg. Nash runs ~250–300 g/day (450–500 g competing).

70–120gper meal is fine

Eat it with fat (steak & eggs) so aminos release slowly — lean-only or shakes spike then get burned off.

#1source: whole eggs

Then red meat, liver, chicken/turkey, oily fish, cheese/cottage cheese. Whey last, and only occasionally.

✕The Myth

You can only absorb ~30 g of protein per meal, and there's a 30-minute anabolic window.

✓Nash

The body uses it all. Nubret ate 100–150 g per meal. Protein synthesis stays elevated 24–48 h and protein digests over 6–10 h — the "window" is a marketing story.

Carbohydrates

Not Essential — Keep Them Low

There is no such thing as an essential carbohydrate (only essential amino acids and fatty acids). The body makes the glucose it needs via gluconeogenesis, and muscle glycogen refills over the week before you train that muscle again.

If you eat them

Under 50–150 g/day, timed around training, zero on rest days. Sources: sweet potato, rice, fruit. Only ~10–12% of people stay lean on high carb.

Sugar is out

Dopamine hit, insulin spike then crash, fat storage, suppressed GH. Avoid sugar, bread, pasta, cereal and "healthy" processed carbs alike.

✕The Myth

You need carbs for energy and to refill glycogen after training.

✓Nash

Glycogen isn't the same as carbs (1 g carb holds ~3 g water). A session burns ~100–150 g and it refills on its own over the week. The post-workout carb rush largely blunts growth hormone.

Fats & Cholesterol

Fat Doesn't Make You Fat

The reframe Insulin makes you fat — not dietary fat.

Fat is essential: testosterone is built from cholesterol, and the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K need it. Cutting fat too low is, in his view, the #1 diet mistake and a fast track to low testosterone.

Cook & choose well

Cook with butter, lard, coconut oil; olive oil raw or low-heat only. Avoid seed & vegetable oils (sunflower, corn, soybean) — they oxidise. Get omega-3 from oily fish or fish oil. Don't over-eat fat either.

The markers that matter

Cholesterol isn't the villain — particle type is. Watch triglyceride : HDL (aim < 2, ideally < 1) and fasting insulin (his sits around 1). Low-carb raises the LDL number but improves the ratios.

Meal Frequency & Timing

Two or Three Meals — Not Six

Fewer meals mean fewer insulin spikes — which keeps insulin sensitivity high and lets growth hormone, autophagy and fat-burning do their work. Eat when hungry, not by the clock; total daily protein matters more than how you split it.

✕The Myth

Eat 6 small meals to "stoke your metabolism."

✓Nash

That came from the food industry. 2–3 meals; 3 on training days, 2 on rest days. Don't eat late — it raises overnight insulin and cortisol.

Intermittent Fasting & Ramadan

A Tool, Not Magic

Intermittent fasting

A 12–16 h fast / 8–12 h window; Nash himself fasts 12–14 h. It won't cost you muscle if total daily protein is high and training is on point. Skip breakfast and train fasted if you like — the glycogen was banked days ago. Not for very-high-calorie needs or beginners.

Ramadan

You can hold or even build muscle. Train 30–60 min before breaking the fast (or 1–2 h after), cut volume ~20–25%, and drink 35–45 ml/kg water a day with electrolytes.

The Grocery List

What to Eat, What to Drop

✚ Eat freely

  • Whole eggs (5–10/day)
  • Red meat & mince
  • Liver & organ meats
  • Oily fish — salmon, tuna, sardines
  • Chicken (legs with skin over breast)
  • Cottage cheese, cheese, full-fat dairy
  • Butter, lard, coconut oil
  • Optional: big green salads & low-carb veg

✕ Drop

  • Sugar & sweets
  • Bread, pasta, rice, cereal, oats, grains
  • Fruit juice & sugary drinks
  • Seed & vegetable oils
  • Processed / packaged food
  • Excess milk
  • Fibre for its own sake — not essential

On the food pyramid: the 1992 USDA "6–11 servings of grains, fat at the top" model — rooted in 1977 low-fat policy — is, in his words, "fundamentally wrong," and the driver of modern obesity and diabetes. The problem is chronic insulin, not calories.

Supplements

Food First — Then These Few

On creatine It works, it's safe — and you may not need it.

If you eat enough red meat and fish (≈4–5 g creatine per kg), you're likely covered. If you supplement: 3–5 g/day, no loading phase (loading is a myth that just upsets your stomach). It really helps vegetarians, vegans and low-red-meat eaters. It doesn't harm healthy kidneys; the weight it adds is intracellular water, not fat.

His top 3

Vitamin D3 4,000–5,000 IU + K2 (MK-7) 100–200 mcg + magnesium ~400 mg, with a fatty meal · Omega-3 (fish / cod-liver oil, EPA+DHA) · Vitamin C 500 mg ×3.

Electrolytes

Essential on low carb: sodium 3–5 g/day (≈7–12 g Celtic/Himalayan salt), potassium 2.6–3.5 g, magnesium ~400 mg. Plain water alone flushes minerals.

Coffee

Fine — 1–3 cups/day, morning and pre-workout, but not within 6–8 h of bed. The cortisol bump is temporary (~60 min). Fat-burner pills, meanwhile, don't work.

Fat Loss & Hormones

Lose Fat, Not Weight

Fat loss ≠ weight loss

Ignore the scale and BMI — judge by the mirror, photos and composition. Crash diets shed >50% muscle and rebound. The method: high protein + low carb + weights + walking + sleep. Low insulin lets glucagon release fat, and most fat is actually burned during sleep. Stay lean year-round — don't bulk; "get fat then get lean is stupid."

The hormone hierarchy

Five levers govern growth: testosterone, insulin sensitivity, cortisol, growth hormone, thyroid. The base of the pyramid is your metabolic environment — insulin sensitivity — then testosterone / GH / nitric oxide, and only at the top do protein, calories and supplements matter. Most people build that pyramid upside-down.

"You are not what you eat — you are what you absorb."

Appendix

The Blueprints He Cites

The Golden-Era diets and methods Nash points to as proof — context, not prescriptions.

Serge Nubret

Up to ~6 lb of meat a day, 400–600 g protein across 2–3 meals, low carb, no supplements, no vegetables. Trained huge volume with ~30 s rest and never to failure. Always lean.

Vince Gironda — "the Iron Guru"

Steak & eggs (½ lb steak + 3–4 eggs in butter, 3–4×/day); "egg & cream" for hardgainers; a carb refeed every 3–4 days (70–100 g). Desiccated liver tablets. "Bodybuilding is 85% nutrition."

Arnold & Sergio Oliva

Protein-dominant, low carb, and no carbs before a show — decades before it was fashionable. Proof, to Nash, that the classic physiques were built on this template.

Nash, at a glance

64 years old · trains 5×/week, once per muscle · ~250–300 g protein and ~2–3 meals a day · fasts 12–14 h · fasting insulin ≈ 1 · "never a serious injury" in 40+ years. He positions his own once-a-week, high-volume, low-carb system as the natural lifter's distillation of everything above.

Read this before you change anything

This page is an editorial summary of one coach's opinions as expressed across his YouTube videos from July 2024 to July 2026 — not medical, dietary or training advice, and not an endorsement.

Several positions here are contrarian versus mainstream sports-science and dietary guidance: protein at 2–3 g/kg sits above the usual 1.6–2.2 g/kg recommendations; "carbs are non-essential / near-zero," "calories matter less than hormones," dismissing the per-meal protein ceiling, and doing zero conventional cardio are all debated. Specific mechanisms and figures he cites (e.g. "90% of growth," exact hormone claims) are his interpretation, not settled consensus.

His own advice also evolved across the window — training volume drifted up (~10–20 → 20–30 sets) and meal frequency down (5–6 → 2–3 meals). Where sources differed, the most-repeated recent position was taken as the through-line.

Talk to a qualified doctor or dietitian before making big training or nutrition changes, especially if you have any medical condition.
