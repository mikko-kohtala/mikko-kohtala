Run Every Set Like This

Straight sets

Same weight for all working sets — reps fall naturally as you fatigue (e.g. 12, 10, 8). Never pyramid up.

Leave 1–2 in the tank

Stop just shy of failure on every set. At most one all-out set on the last exercise of a muscle, occasionally.

Tempo 3–4s

~1–2s up, 2s+ down. Control the negative, no bouncing, no lockout — keep constant tension.

Rest short

Upper body 30–60s, isolation 30–45s, legs 60–120s. Enough to hold form, not enough to fully recover.

Order the day

Lead with the heaviest barbell compound, then dumbbells and machines, isolation last. Warm-up ramps don't count as sets.

Add weight when earned

Beat the top of the rep range on set 1 in clean form → add ~5% (2.5 kg) next time. Reps reset to the bottom.

Running It Long-Term

How To Progress

Muscle grows in waves, not a straight line. Don't add weight for the sake of it — earn it.

The double-progression rule Beat the top of the rep range, then — and only then — add weight.

Keep the same load until set 1 hits the top number in clean form (12 upper body, 20 for quads). Next session, add ~5% (about 2.5 kg) and let the reps drop back to the bottom of the range. Repeat. This is slow on purpose: it adds up to real muscle over months and years.

  1. Start light enough to hit the top of the range on your first working set with a rep or two still in reserve.
  2. Chase reps first. Same weight every session until set 1 reaches the top number cleanly across the whole exercise.
  3. Then add ~2.5 kg. Reps reset to the bottom of the range — that's expected. Rebuild them week by week.
  4. Stuck for 2–3 weeks? Don't force load. Add a rep, slow the negative, add a set, or tighten form before touching the weight.
  5. Change little, and rarely. Swap barbell↔dumbbell versions or reorder a lift every few months — keep a working routine 3–6 months.
  6. Guard recovery. 7–9 h sleep, once-per-muscle frequency, and the low-carb high-protein plan in the Method Guide do the growing.

New to lifting? Don't start here. Build up with full-body training 3×/week for a few months, then upper/lower, and only then move to the once-a-week splits — beginning near the bottom of every set range (10–12 sets per big muscle) and adding volume over time toward these targets.

A note before you start

These routines are a faithful, practical build of the training method Dr. Nash Jocic describes across his 2024–2026 videos — exercise choices, set/rep ranges, rest, tempo and progression all follow his stated principles. Set counts were arranged to land inside his per-muscle volume ranges; he never dictates one fixed sheet, so treat this as a starting template to adjust to your own recovery.

The very high weekly volume here (20–30 sets for big muscles) is Nash's position and sits above what much of the research uses — build up to it gradually rather than jumping straight in, and pair it with the nutrition and recovery in the Method Guide. This is a summary of one coach's approach, not medical or coaching advice — check with a qualified professional before starting, especially with any injury or medical condition.
