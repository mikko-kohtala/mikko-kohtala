Five-day split for advanced lifters

A higher-focus version of the Nash method: one muscle emphasis per session, two weekly rest days, high-quality volume, and no twice-per-week muscle frequency.

5 sessions Use this when recovery, time, joints, and consistency are already proven.

Use Criteria

Nash presents the four-day split as the default. This five-day version is for a smaller group of advanced lifters.

Choose it if

You can recover from focused volume

  • You can train five days consistently without rushing sessions.
  • Your joints are not irritated by current volume.
  • Your sleep and food are stable enough to support harder sessions.
  • You benefit from giving chest, back, legs, shoulders, and arms their own focus.
Do not choose it if

You are already under-recovered

  • Your elbows, shoulders, knees, or lower back are persistently sore.
  • You need long rests just to survive the work.
  • You are adding volume while eating or sleeping poorly.
  • You are a beginner who has not built exercise skill yet.
Core rule: five days does not mean training a muscle twice per week. The added day gives more room for arms and single-muscle focus while preserving weekly recovery.

Progression Rules

The same rules apply as the four-day split; the difference is more focus per session.

Working weight

Keep it stable

Use straight sets after warm-ups. Do not pyramid every exercise into one heavy set.

Add load

Only when earned

When the first set exceeds the top rep target with strict form, add roughly 5% next time.

Rest density

Do not drift

If rest becomes 3-5 minutes, the session has shifted toward strength training.

Deload signal

Joints decide

Persistent joint pain, sleep disruption, and falling motivation mean reduce optional sets first.
